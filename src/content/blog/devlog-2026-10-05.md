Every project has weeks where the graph is flat. This was one of those weeks.

This is the first entry in a weekly devlog for my current main project — a browser-based 3D car simulation built with Three.js and Cannon-es: vehicle physics, a test track, and cinematic camera modes. The goal I've committed to is generating tracks for five scenarios, each featuring ten different cars.

## The honest update

No real progress this week. The blocker wasn't technical — it was attention. I kept drifting toward other projects that felt easier or more exciting to start, and the car simulation stayed exactly where it was: untouched.

**Feeling:** stuck — and distracted. No screenshots to show this time.

## Why I'm logging a "nothing" week anyway

Before starting this project I wrote a focus contract: a one-page commitment that says I don't switch projects until the finish criteria are met — unless I go through a deliberate, non-impulsive abandon process. Part of the agreement is that I show up every week and write down what happened, even when the update is just "nothing much."

There's a reason for that rule. Progress that's visible is fuel against distraction. A blank week feels like it disappears; a written week — even a stuck one — keeps the loop honest, and makes it easier to pick things back up next week.

## The smallest next step

The contract's principle: the smallest real step counts. So next week's goal isn't finishing the demo — it's one scenario, one track, a handful of cars rendering end-to-end. If even that feels heavy, it's just opening the project and pressing play.

Stuck weeks are part of the process. Logging this one still counts. See you next week.
